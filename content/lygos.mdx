For a payment platform, the interface is the product. A confusing form is a lost sale for the merchant, and a payment without a clear status is a support ticket, or worse, a customer who thinks he just lost his money.

<Card title="our vision" content="Make getting paid as simple as sharing a link. A merchant should go from sign up to his first payment in minutes, follow every transaction live and never wonder where his money is." />

## Concept

Lygos served two people at the same time: the merchant who sells and the customer who pays. Each one needed his own experience, and both had to trust it from the first screen.

Here's the journey of a merchant:
- Sign up with a Mobile Money number and activate the account with a code sent by email
- Set up the shop: category, type of activity, website and address
- Create a payment link with pictures, a price in local currency and a promotional price
- Share the link, the customer pays on the checkout with his Mobile Money operator
- Follow the revenue and every transaction from the dashboard

Signing up asked for the Mobile Money number first. Lygos bet on what works where it did business, and in French-speaking Africa people mainly pay and get paid with their phone. The activation code came with the two exits people need when an email is late: resend the code or contact support.

## Craft

### Payment links

The payment link was the heart of Lygos. A merchant filled a form and got a page he could share anywhere, on WhatsApp, Instagram or his own website.

<Image src="/images/projects/lygos/payment-link.webp" width="672" height="450" alt="The payment link editor with an image uploader, the product name and the price in XAF"/>

**Edit and preview** - Two tabs, one form. The merchant switched to the preview to see exactly what his customer would see before publishing anything.

**Pictures** - Drag and drop up to 8 images of 5MB each, with a thumbnail and a way to remove each one. The file uploader became a shared component we reused across the products.

**Pricing** - The price was set in local currency, with a tool to calculate the rates by country and an optional promotional price.

**Rich descriptions** - A rich text editor with a live preview, so merchants could describe their products properly and convert more visitors into buyers.

### Checkout

The checkout is where the money moves, so every detail counts. The customer entered his name, email and Mobile Money number, picked his operator, could apply a coupon and saw the final price before paying.

<Image src="/images/projects/lygos/checkout.webp" width="672" height="460" alt="A Lygos checkout for a Photoshop masterclass, with the payment form, the operator choice and a coupon applied"/>

The checkout form and the discount code manager were part of a component kit I built for payments. Each component was reused across the products and covered by automated tests, because a bug in a checkout is never a small bug.

### Real-time payment status

Mobile Money payments are not instant. The customer confirms on his phone, the operator answers, sometimes late. Asking customers to refresh a page to know if they paid was not an option.

With the backend team, we designed a transaction status page that received every update with **Server-Sent Events**. The server talks, the browser listens. We only needed one direction, so it was simpler than WebSockets, and the browser reconnects on its own when the network drops, which happens a lot on mobile.

### Merchant dashboard

Merchants want one answer when they log in: how much did I make? Inspired by Stripe, the dashboard showed the total revenue, today's revenue, the latest orders and charts of the revenue by week, month and year.

### Shop setup

To verify a merchant, Lygos needed to know who he was and where he sold. The address field used Google Places autocomplete with a map, so merchants picked their real location in a couple of keystrokes instead of typing it by hand.

<Image src="/images/projects/lygos/shop-setup.webp" width="672" height="416" alt="The shop setup form with the category, the type of activity, the shop URL and an address field suggesting Bafoussam, Cameroon"/>

### For developers

Lygos also served developers who integrated payments with its API, and they needed a safe place to try before going live.

**Sandbox** - I built the sandbox experience in the dashboard. Developers used test API keys and simulated successful, failed and pending payments with fake data that never reached production.

**Webhooks** - From the Configurations page, a developer added an HTTPS endpoint and picked the events he wanted for payment links, gateway deposits and marketplaces, whether a payment was submitted, completed or failed. Each webhook came with a secret to verify the signature of every request, and the secret could be regenerated from the dashboard.

### Back office

The team needed its own tools to keep the platform safe. I built the internal admin tool to review merchant profiles and investigate transactions, and shipped it faster with Claude Code.

## Engineering

The product grew fast, so the frontend had to stay easy to change for anyone who joined the team.

**Architecture** - I documented the frontend architecture with diagrams and ADRs (Architecture Decision Records), so every important choice came with the reason behind it.

**Coding style** - Our conventions were written down, which kept every pull request consistent and made code reviews about the product, not about the format.

**Performance and refactoring** - Optimizations and refactorings were documented too, so the reasoning stayed long after the change was merged.

**Testing** - End-to-end tests with Playwright covered the flows that couldn't break: register, login, creating a payment link and a client checking out. They ran on top of the automated tests of the shared components.

**CI/CD** - Pipelines on GitHub checked every pull request and shipped the apps to production.

**Translation** - The apps spoke several languages with next-intl.

## Stats

In 7 months, active users grew by **200%**, driven by a simpler and clearer experience. At its peak, Lygos powered more than **15,000 businesses** selling locally and internationally.
